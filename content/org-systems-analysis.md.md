## Primary Resources

The primary resources in Wikipedia are encyclopedic articles whose purpose is to summarize knowledge across a wide variety of topics. Each article contains the following:

* Textual content - Explanations, definitions, and narratives about the subject of the article.
* Images and media - Photographs and videos that visually support the textual content.
* References - Citations to external sources that influenced the textual content.
* Data - Tables and info boxes that summarize key facts, like dates or statistics.
## Primary Interactions

Wikipedia is designed to support a wide variety of user interactions, including the following:

* Research - Students, professionals, and casual learners can use Wikipedia to gather information or reliable sources quickly for their research.
* Browsing - Relationships between resources encourage exploration, allowing users to move from one article to another.
* Fact checking - The textual content can provide quick answers to questions, like dates of events or definitions of terms.
* Contribution - Users are able to edit and expand articles to add necessary information, supporting the website's community driven system.
* Discussion - Talk pages allow contributors to debate edits and sources, supporting collaboration in the article creation process.
## Classification System

Wikipedia uses a hybrid classification system, using both hierarchical and faceted classification:

* Hierarchical - Articles are grouped into categories, and those categories are grouped into subcategories. For instance, the user can go from the broad "Science" article, then to the "Natural Science" article through the article series, then to the "Branches of Natural Science" category in that article, and then to the "Astronomy" subcategory in that category.
* Faceted - Articles can belong to multiple categories or articles at the same time. For instance, "Astronomy" is grouped under the "Natural Science" article, as well as the "Mathematics", "Physics", and "Chemistry" article.

This hybrid system is effective for Wikipedia's large and diverse audience, as hierarchies help users who prefer a fixed structure throughout navigation, while facets helps users find topics from multiple paths of navigation.
## Relationships Between Resources

Relationships between resources are represented in multiple ways, including the following:

* Hyperlinks - Links that connect related articles directly within the textual content, allowing for easy user exploration.
* See also sections - Lists of related articles that can guide users to similar topics.
* Categories - Articles can be tagged with categories that group them into a collection, like "Science" or "Mathematics".
* Info boxes - Structured data tables that summarize key facts of the article and link to related articles.
* References section - Contains external links, connecting the articles to the sources that influenced the textual content and reinforcing the credibility of the article.
